# Security Policy

ScamHunter is my project for researching harmful sites and helping people understand online risks. Getting more reports isn't worth putting anyone's safety or privacy at risk.

## Stay safe while researching

- Treat suspicious links, pages, attachments, and downloads as untrusted.
- Don't enter real passwords, personal details, or payment info.
- Don't download or run unknown files on your everyday device.
- Keep your browser and operating system updated. Don't turn off security protections.
- Don't break into accounts, bypass access controls, exploit vulnerabilities, or disrupt websites.
- Keep interactions with suspected malicious sites to safe, passive observation.
- Never publish passwords, session tokens, private information, or details that identify victims.
- If a task genuinely needs a VM, disable shared folders and clipboard, avoid unnecessary USB access, and isolate networking carefully. A VM is an extra layer, not a guarantee of containment.

## Found a problem in ScamHunter itself?

If you find a security or privacy issue in this project, please don't post exploitable details or private data publicly. Contact me through the safest available private channel on GitHub. Include a short description, the possible impact, and safe steps to reproduce it—without exposing real users or secrets.

## Evidence matters

A domain name, certificate, hosting provider, registration date, or reputation score by itself doesn't prove harm. Write down what the evidence actually shows and what it can't tell you. Automated detections are a reason to take a closer look, not proof on their own.

## Reporting other sites

For suspected phishing, fraud, malware, or other harmful websites, use the appropriate official reporting service. The [README](README.md#where-to-report-a-site-or-incident) has some starting points. Check each organization's current instructions before sending a report.
