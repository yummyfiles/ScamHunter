# Sources and Research Tools

This is the ScamHunter toolbox: places to check public threat reports, research indicators, protect your connection, and report harmful activity. No single tool proves a site is safe or harmful, so record what each source actually says and when you checked it.

## Check a URL or threat feed

- [Google Safe Browsing](https://transparencyreport.google.com/safe-browsing/search) — check whether Google has flagged a URL.
- [VirusTotal](https://www.virustotal.com/) — checks URL, domain, IP, and file reputation using multiple security engines.
- [URLhaus](https://urlhaus.abuse.ch/) — URLs reported as being used to distribute malware.
- [PhishTank](https://phishtank.org/) — community-submitted phishing data.
- [ThreatFox](https://threatfox.abuse.ch/) — community-shared malware indicators; check the indicator type, context, and confidence.
- [OpenPhish](https://www.openphish.com/) — phishing intelligence and reporting.
- [ICANN Lookup](https://lookup.icann.org/) — registration details for domains, where available. Registration data alone doesn't prove wrongdoing.

## Malware protection and analysis

- [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/) — consumer malware protection and scanning tools.
- [Microsoft Defender](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/comprehensive-security) — built-in Windows security tools and protection information.
- [Hybrid Analysis](https://www.hybrid-analysis.com/) — public malware-analysis reports and sandbox analysis.
- [ANY.RUN](https://any.run/) — interactive malware sandbox and analysis reports.
- [CyberChef](https://gchq.github.io/CyberChef/) — local-in-browser transformations and decoding for data you already have a legitimate reason to inspect.
- [VirtualBox](https://www.virtualbox.org/), [KVM/QEMU](https://www.qemu.org/), and [GNOME Boxes](https://apps.gnome.org/Boxes/) — options for running a separate research environment when one is actually needed.

**Safety note:** Don't download suspicious files just to investigate them, and don't run malware on your everyday computer. If you need a VM, disable shared folders and clipboard, avoid unnecessary USB access, keep snapshots, and isolate networking carefully. A VM is an extra layer, not a guarantee that malware can't escape. Public sandboxes and scanners may share submitted files, URLs, and results with other users or security partners. Never upload private documents, personal images, credentials, or confidential URLs without understanding the service's data-sharing policy.

## Privacy tools

- [Tor Browser](https://www.torproject.org/download/) — routes browsing through the Tor network to help reduce tracking and hide your IP address from the destination site. Download it only from the official Tor Project site.
- [Proton VPN](https://protonvpn.com/)
- [Mullvad VPN](https://mullvad.net/)
- [IVPN](https://www.ivpn.net/)

A VPN mainly protects the connection between your device and the VPN provider; it does not make you anonymous by itself or make malicious sites safe. Tor and VPNs have different designs and trade-offs, so don't assume using both automatically improves privacy. Review the provider's current privacy policy, limitations, and pricing before relying on a service.

## Personal-data and image-abuse support

- [Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com/) — check whether your own email address appears in known data breaches. Don't use it to investigate other people's accounts without permission.
- [StopNCII.org](https://stopncii.org/) — support for eligible adults dealing with non-consensual intimate image sharing.
- [Take It Down](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/) — helps people prevent or remove the online sharing of eligible intimate images taken when they were under 18. Use the official service and don't download or forward images to submit them.

For exposed personal data or non-consensual images, avoid copying or reposting the material. Record only the minimum necessary public references and use the affected platform's reporting/removal process.

## Report a site or incident

- [Google Safe Browsing — report phishing](https://safebrowsing.google.com/safebrowsing/report_phish/)
- [Microsoft — report an unsafe site](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/wdsi/support/report-unsafe-site)
- [FTC ReportFraud](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) — fraud in the US
- [FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3)](https://www.ic3.gov/) — internet crime reports in the US
- [CISA reporting](https://www.cisa.gov/report) — cybersecurity incidents and issues

## Keep in mind

- A feed listing is a lead or a source's finding, not automatic proof of every claim about a site.
- Not being on a blocklist doesn't mean a website is safe.
- Don't open harmful links, interact with criminal services, bypass access controls, or download suspicious files just for research.
- Don't submit private or sensitive URLs to third-party scanners without checking their data-handling policies.
- Don't publish victims' personal information, credentials, or copies of non-consensual images.
- Record the source, date checked, what it actually reported, and what ScamHunter has independently verified.
- Check each service's current terms, policies, and official instructions before using it.
